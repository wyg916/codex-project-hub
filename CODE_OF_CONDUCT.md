# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want participation in Codex Project Hub to be safe, respectful, and useful. Maintainers, contributors, and users are expected to treat one another with patience and professional courtesy regardless of experience, identity, background, language, or point of view.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and code on their merits.
- Give specific, actionable feedback without personal attacks.
- Assume good faith while still asking for evidence.
- Respect privacy, confidentiality, licensing, and security boundaries.
- Make space for questions and acknowledge mistakes.
- Stop or redirect a discussion when a maintainer asks for de-escalation.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory language.
- Sexualized attention or imagery.
- Trolling, repeated disruption, or deliberate misinformation.
- Publishing another person's private information or repository data without permission.
- Pressuring anyone to disclose credentials, private communications, or personal details.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a conduct or security concern.
- Using project spaces to promote unrelated commercial, political, or personal campaigns.

## Scope

This policy applies to project repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and project-related public or private communication. It also applies when someone represents the project at an event or in another community.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately through the repository owner's private GitHub contact method. Do not post sensitive incident evidence in a public issue. Include the relevant links, dates, participants, and a factual description; redact unrelated personal information.

Maintainers will limit disclosure to the people needed to review and address the report. Conflicts of interest should be declared, and a different reviewer should handle the report when practical.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and history, maintainers may issue a clarification, warning, temporary participation restriction, content removal, or permanent ban. Immediate restrictions may be applied when needed to protect people or private data. Enforcement decisions should be documented privately and applied consistently.

Good-faith disagreement, a rejected proposal, or evidence-based technical criticism is not by itself a conduct violation.
